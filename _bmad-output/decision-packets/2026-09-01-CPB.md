## 3. What it settled

*Questions it answered, each with the figure that answered it.*

- **Question 3 (is the price already too rich?) — yes, on the evidence
  available.** The reverse DCF shows today's price implies **14.9%/year**
  revenue growth for 5 years (at ThesisTrace's 10% discount-rate assumption),
  against **7.3%/year actually achieved, FY2017–2025**. I pulled the actual
  year-by-year revenue to check whether that 8-year average was hiding a
  recent acceleration: FY2017 $5.84B → FY2018 $6.62B (+13.3%) → FY2019 $8.11B
  (+22.6%) → FY2020 $8.69B (+7.2%) → FY2021 $8.48B (-2.5%) → FY2022 $8.56B
  (+1.0%) → FY2023 $9.36B (+9.3%) → FY2024 $9.64B (+3.0%) → FY2025 $10.25B
  (+6.4%). It isn't hiding one — FY2024 (the year the Sovos Brands acquisition
  closed) was one of the weakest growth years in the table, and FY2025 (the
  first full year with Sovos Brands consolidated) came in at 6.4%, *below*
  the 8-year average, not above it. Nothing in the recent, post-acquisition
  data supports the growth rate the current price requires.

## 4. Where it ran out

*Each blocking gap, phrased as a question the product could not answer.*

- Which specific signals actually drove the Piotroski F-Score's move from 4
  (FY2024, "Middle") to 7 (FY2025, "Middle") — and did the debt-related signal
  I actually cared about (given the Sovos Brands acquisition debt) improve, or
  did other, unrelated signals mask it? The Financial Health tab shows the raw
  signal list for each year (met / not met / insufficient data) but nothing
  that diffs two years or names what changed between them. I had to manually
  compare both years' full signal lists by hand to find that `leverage_decreasing`
  ("the long-term debt ratio decreased year over year") failed in **both**
  FY2024 and FY2025 — the score's improvement was driven entirely by
  `asset_turnover_increasing`, `current_ratio_increasing`, and `roa_increasing`
  newly passing, none of which are about the debt load. "Recovered" was the
  wrong read of the headline number; the debt-ratio signal never actually
  moved. Without a "what changed" diff, the product's own headline
  (Middle → Middle, ↑ Improving) reads as reassurance about the acquisition
  debt specifically, when it isn't one.

- Was CPB's leverage actually declining across multiple years, or just moving
  between two adjacent ones? The Financial Health tab only ever compares
  consecutive fiscal years ("→ Broadly stable FY2024 → FY2025"), never the
  full multi-year shape. I had to manually expand three separate Altman
  Z-Score year-cards (FY2023, FY2024, FY2025) and line up
  `x4_market_value_equity` by hand to see the equity cushion has fallen every
  year for three straight years (0.988 → 0.731 → 0.526), and the Z-score
  itself has been declining the whole time (2.624 → 1.891 → 1.850). FY2025's
  "Broadly stable" label is really "stable at the bottom of a three-year
  slide" — which reads very differently from a recovery. A trend-line view
  across all available years, per model, would have shown this shape without
  the manual reconstruction.
